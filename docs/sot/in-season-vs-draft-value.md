# In-Season vs Draft Value

## Why draft capital matters all season

### Waiver priority and FAAB
- Strong drafts = fewer roster holes = you can be aggressive on the wire for upside, not desperation.
- Weak drafts = spending FAAB/waiver on replacement-level players instead of league-winners.

### Trade leverage
- Good draft picks are trade assets. A round-5 steal can fetch a round-3 quality player mid-season.
- Bad draft picks have negative trade value — you're stuck cutting or benching.

### Mental and strategic load
- Playing catch-up from week 1 changes decision-making: more risky trades, overpaying on waivers, chasing points.
- Consistent playoff teams often win in the draft, not just on the wire.

## The catch-up trap
Teams that miss on rounds 1-3 often:
1. Reach on waivers early (overpay for hot week).
2. Trade future value for present need.
3. Start inferior players because of sunk cost.

## Draft goal
Enter week 1 with:
- 2 RBs you're confident starting.
- 2 WRs you're confident starting.
- Flex option with upside.
- QB/TE/DST/K that don't require weekly panic.

If your draft doesn't meet this checklist, you are already behind a team that did.

## Measuring draft success
Compare your draft grade to final standing year over year. Correlation > 0.5 is common in competitive leagues — draft quality predicts season outcome.
