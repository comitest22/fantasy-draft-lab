# Draft Slot Strategy (Snake)

2026 consensus (FantasyPros, Fantasy Six Pack, Draft Sharks): every seat still wants **at least one RB in the first three**. Late seats can open WR-WR, but round 3 should not leave you RB-less unless the board is absurd.

## Pick 1-3 (Early)
- **Strength:** First crack at elite talent; back-to-back in round 2.
- **Strategy:** Take best RB/WR available. Use the turn to grab two starters (e.g., RB + WR or WR + WR in PPR), then make sure a running back is on the roster by pick three.
- **Avoid:** Reaching for QB/TE at the turn because of a long wait — only if elite value. Rigid Zero RB from this seat wastes the RB scarcity.

## Pick 4-7 (Middle)
- **Strength:** Flexibility — can react to the board. Often get a tier drop at the turn.
- **Strategy:** Don't lock into a plan. Let the board fall, then attack value at the turn. If you opened WR, prioritize an RB at the 2/3 turn.
- **Avoid:** Predicting who will be there — have 3-4 targets for each pick.

## Pick 8-10 (Late)
- **Strength:** Three picks in rounds 1-3 (10-team: e.g. 8, 13, 20 depending on slot).
- **Strategy:** Anchor with two elite players at the turn. WR-WR is a live floor opening from here; still take an RB by round 3 if a workhorse is on the board. An elite RB falling is the ceiling path.
- **Avoid:** Reaching for a third WR when an RB1/RB2 is the last defined role in the range. Still wait on QB.

## Snake math reminder
- Round 2 pick 10 in a 10-team = overall pick 11.
- Track **overall pick number**, not just round — ADP is in overall picks.

## Turn planning
Before draft, list:
1. Your ideal pick at current slot.
2. Fallback if a tier break happens.
3. Position you'd take at the turn if a run occurs — including the Hero RB constraint (one RB in the first three).
